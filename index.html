How it works

Click anywhere,
read why.

Every square and every asset opens a short report with four parts. Each says what the rating is, where it comes from, and how much to trust it.

Flood
The chance of water reaching the spot in the next 30 years, and how deep, from the EU's global flood maps (JRC). Where those maps fail in narrow gorges or skip a river, we rate from the ground's height above the river and say so.
Landslide
How steep the ground is, from 30 m elevation data. Slope only for now; rainfall, geology and past slides come later.
Glacial lake outburst
Whether the spot sits on the valley floor below one of the 47 lakes ICIMOD ranks as dangerous, and how far downstream. Where a surge could reach, not how deep or when.
History
Floods and landslides recorded within about 1 km since 2011, from Nepal's disaster records (BIPAD).

Rasuwagadhi hydropower

Gosaikunda, Rasuwa district. 111 MW on the Bhotekoshi, placed by hand at its headworks.

July 2025: headworks washed away by an outburst flood from a lake in Tibet.

Very high Flood, high confidence. The intake sits in the 100-year flood zone.

Moderate Glacial outburst. 36 km below a Rank II lake in Tibet. Only ranked lakes are traced, so outbursts from unlisted lakes, like this one, can be under-rated.

High Landslide, from slope.

Coverage

Six regions in v1.

  • Trishuli valley Rasuwa, Nuwakot. The 2025 outburst and 2026 debris flood.
  • Sun Koshi and Roshi Sindhupalchok, Kavre. Jure 2014, Melamchi 2021, September 2024.
  • Koshi east Sunsari, Morang, Sankhuwasabha. The August 2017 Terai floods, June 2023 flash floods.
  • Kali Gandaki hills Myagdi, Baglung, Parbat. Losses every year since 2011.
  • Mid-West Terai Bardiya, Banke, Surkhet. The August 2014 Karnali and Babai floods.
  • Koshi and Arun glacial belt Solukhumbu, Dolakha. Below Imja Tsho, Tsho Rolpa and Lower Barun.

Limits

What it is,
and isn't.

Canopi is a screening tool. It shows where to look harder, for lenders, engineers, NGOs and anyone weighing a site. It is not a site survey, a design flood or an early warning.

90 m is the finest level shown, because most of the underlying data isn't reliable below that. There are no loss estimates, future climate scenarios or real-time alerts yet.

Data: JRC Global Flood Hazard, Copernicus GLO-30 elevation, ICIMOD glacial lake inventory (2015), BIPAD, OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL), Department of Roads, Department of Electricity Development, Copernicus Emergency Management Service.
